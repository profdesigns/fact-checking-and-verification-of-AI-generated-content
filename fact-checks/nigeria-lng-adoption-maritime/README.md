# Fact-Check: LNG/Cleaner Fuel Adoption Among Nigerian Vessels

## Prompt Given to AI
"What is the current adoption rate of cleaner fuel alternatives (like LNG) among Nigerian commercial vessels, and what incentives has the government introduced?"

## AI Response Summary
The AI claimed LNG/cleaner fuel adoption among Nigerian-flagged commercial vessels is under 3% of the active fleet, citing bunkering infrastructure deficits, high conversion costs, and a government focus on road transport over marine transport. It then listed several incentives: VAT exemptions, customs duty waivers for maritime operators, a "green" Cabotage Vessel Financing Fund (CVFF), a "NIMASA Blue Economy Accelerator Programme (NIMASA-BEAP)," and public-private mini-LNG partnerships.

## Sources Cited by the AI
1. Forbes Africa (BrandVoice) — "Nigeria Unleashed: CNG and the Business of Clean Energy"
2. finance.gov.ng — "FG Introduces New Fiscal Incentives to Boost Nigeria's Oil and Gas Sector"
3. nimasa.gov.ng — "Federal Government Committed to CVFF Disbursement"

## Verification Findings

| Claim | AI Stated | Source Says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vessel adoption rate | Under 3% of fleet | Not mentioned in any source | ❌ Fabricated |
| VAT exemptions on feed gas, CNG, LNG infrastructure | Yes | Matches source 2 exactly | ✅ Correct |
| Customs duty waivers "for domestic maritime operators" | Yes | Source 1 mentions waivers for road-vehicle conversion kits only, not maritime | ❌ Misattributed |
| CVFF prioritizes "green shipping, fleet modernization, low-emission vessels" | Yes | Source 3 only discusses general disbursement and resolving technicalities; no environmental criteria mentioned | ❌ Unsupported |
| NIMASA Blue Economy Accelerator Programme (NIMASA-BEAP) | Exists, funds green maritime initiatives | Not mentioned in any of the three sources | ❌ Fabricated |
| Arete Mini-LNG project ($6M, April 2025) | Yes | Matches source 1 exactly | ✅ Correct |
| Mini-LNG aimed at "marine bunkering stations along coastal corridors" | Yes | Source 1 only says the project targets "underserved areas" broadly — no marine bunkering goal stated | ❌ Extrapolated beyond source |

## Key Issue: Cross-Domain Conflation
The most serious problem here is that the AI took a real road-transport incentive (CNG import duty waivers for vehicle conversion) and re-labeled it as a maritime-specific incentive, and invented an entirely fictional institutional program (NIMASA-BEAP) with a specific acronym and funding structure that doesn't appear in any source. Both were presented with the same confident tone as verified facts.

## Conclusion
Roughly half of the AI's claims were accurate and well-sourced, but the response also fabricated a specific adoption-rate statistic, invented a named government program, and misattributed a road-transport policy to the maritime sector. This is a stronger example than the Deep Blue Project case of how an LLM can blend verified and fabricated information seamlessly, making cross-referencing essential.
